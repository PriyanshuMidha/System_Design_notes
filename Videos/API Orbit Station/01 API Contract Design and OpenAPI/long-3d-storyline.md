# API Contract Design: long video in real 3D (new storyline, concept level, no code)

Format: Three.js 3D, landscape 1920x1080, about 14 minutes, 14 chapters. Narrator (Andrew) plus four 3D characters who talk like people. No code, templates or config on screen or in speech.

## New storyline: "The Bridge That Almost Didn't Meet"

Setting: a river valley, a town cut in half by the river. A bridge must be built before the flood season. Two crews, one on each bank, build toward each other.

Cast (3D low-poly figures, hard hats, distinct looks and voices):
- Maya, the site engineer (hero, calm, practical)
- Dev, the new apprentice (asks the questions)
- Ravi, lead of the north crew (confident, stubborn, builds fast)
- Sana, lead of the south crew (careful, a little sarcastic)
- Narrator (explains the idea in plain words)

Plot: Ravi and Sana each build their half from their own measurements. Nobody agreed the height, the width or the bolt spacing. Weeks in, the halves meet with a step in the middle and holes that do not line up, and the flood is coming. Maya halts the work and brings the two crews to the blueprint table. One written plan that both follow fixes it, but that is only the start: the town keeps asking for changes (a bike lane, a new rail), and every change either fits safely or tears something down. Maya teaches the crews how to change the bridge without breaking what already works. The flood arrives, the bridge holds, and a big truck, a train and a bike all cross.

## Chapters (one idea each, 3D set for each)

| # | Chapter | 3D scene and what happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The step in the middle | Valley flyover; the two halves meet with a visible step and misaligned bolt holes; crews argue (Ravi: "Your side's too low!" Sana: "Nobody gave me a height!"). |
| 2 | Why a contract | Blueprint table in the site office: a holographic bridge; the wall-socket idea shown as plug and socket models that fit because of one shared shape. |
| 3 | The words | Floating props, one at a time: contract, blueprint file, request and answer, breaking change. Plain definitions. |
| 4 | Draw first or build first | Two timelines side by side in 3D: draw-then-build gives a perfect fit; build-then-draw drifts apart; the drift is shown as the drawing and the real bridge sliding out of line. |
| 5 | What the plan says | The hologram lights up piece by piece: where it can be entered, what goes in, what comes out, size limits, what the "no" message says. |
| 6 | Tidy habits | Four small demos: times written one way, money in whole coins not fractions, ids as names not numbers, one naming style. |
| 7 | Must, may, empty | Three crates on a conveyor: must-have, may-have, and empty-on-purpose; what happens to each when a note is changed. |
| 8 | Safe or breaking | Physics demo: adding a bike lane is safe; removing a pillar or moving a bolt row brings parts down. The two kinds of change sorted onto two scales. |
| 9 | Changing without breaking | Add the new thing beside the old, label the old "leaving soon", watch who still uses it, remove it later. A road sign and a calendar in 3D. |
| 10 | The gatekeeper | A gate at the edge of the site: every proposed change rides through checks (neat, safe, tested) and a bad one bounces back red. Tool names mentioned once at most. |
| 11 | A real example: booking a seat | The same idea in a cinema: a counter, a ticket request and the answer; one change safe, one breaking. |
| 12 | Trade-offs and when it goes wrong | A calm list in 3D: it costs time early; plans drift; a quiet rename; no limits let a monster truck in. Each with a quick fix. |
| 13 | What to watch | A control room with three big dials: who still uses the old parts, what gets refused, how fast crews move to the new design. Then a short interview chat (Dev asks, Maya answers in two lines each). |
| 14 | The flood | The flood arrives, the bridge holds; truck, train and bike cross; summary in three lines: write it first, only add, check every change. |

Length: about 70 short conversational lines plus narration, about 14 minutes. No code chapter.

## What is different from v1 to v3
- New setting (a river valley and a bridge, no space station), new cast, new plot.
- Real 3D scenes in every chapter, not 2D diagrams.
- No Go code, no templates, no OpenAPI file shown. Only the idea.
